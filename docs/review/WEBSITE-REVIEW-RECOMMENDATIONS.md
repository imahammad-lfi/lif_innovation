# Linkfields website review: what to remove, merge and rework

**Date:** 8 November 2026  
**Scope:** All 77 live pages, desktop (1,440 px) and mobile (390 px), plus navigation, mega menus, mobile drawer, footer, shared sections, forms and SEO  
**Benchmark:** The 2026 corporate deck (positioning, five portfolios, Linkfields IP, value plays, commercial models, global presence)  
**Method:** Thirteen page groups were reviewed section by section, in parallel and read-only, then combined with automated checks for status, length, SEO, links, spelling and repeated text. Detailed group reports are listed in the appendix.

---

## 1. The short answer

You are right: a large share of the sections are not needed. Of the **668 existing sections** assessed, the review recommends **removing 140**, **merging 102** into another section, **moving 30** to a better page and **reworking 356**. Only **40 can stay exactly as they are**. Fourteen of the 77 pages should be **retired or merged** into stronger parent pages with permanent redirects, which brings the site to **63 focused pages**.

The foundations work and should stay: the visual system, the five-portfolio taxonomy, the Products & Accelerators area, the dark "Industries we know deeply" photo grid, the sticky section tabs and the full-globe hero. The problems are elsewhere:

1. **Repetition.** The same blocks are copied across many pages: the eight-industry grid, value plays, "Why Linkfields", "Who we are", the social strip, "Insights & Updates" and the blue CTA strip. Pages end up long and alike. Home is 13,695 px on desktop and 24,724 px on mobile, and some single sub-service pages run to 15–17 sections.
2. **Legacy copy from the old site** remains on about 30 sub-pages. Examples include meta descriptions saying *"We are a leading manufacturing / banking / insurance company"*, text copied from unrelated pages, American spelling (*specialization* ×49) and unsupported numbers such as *"10x faster operational growth"* and *"60% savings"*.
3. **Two competing navigation taxonomies.** The "Solutions" menu (SAP, Odoo, Microsoft Dynamics, Salesforce, iPaaS, RPA, Testorium Z) duplicates Services and leads to legacy `/solutions/...` pages.
4. **Claims without proof.** The site *says* "one strategic partner" and "software we own" but does not *show* it. There are no case studies or product screenshots, and the credentials have no sources.
5. **A few defects that lose enquiries.** The contact form accepts only South African phone numbers, which blocks buyers in the UAE, UK, USA, Australia and India. The Contact page has no H1, and the LinkedIn link opens an admin-only page.

**Depth should come from proof, not more text.** What will make the site stand out is fewer, sharper sections, each backed by evidence: a case, a product screen or a delivery artefact.

## 5. Navigation: one taxonomy, one job per menu

**Problem.** "Solutions" lists vendors and tools (SAP, Odoo, Microsoft Dynamics, Salesforce, iPaaS, RPA) plus an unexplained external "Testorium Z" link. All of these already sit under Services, Products & Accelerators or Industries.

**Recommended structure.** Each menu answers one buyer question:

| Menu | Buyer question | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Services | "What can you do?" | Five portfolios and sub-services, plus a visible "All services" link |
| **Solutions (repurposed)** | "Which of my problems can you solve?" | The deck's five strategic value plays: **AI Code Assurance**, **Modernisation & Programme Assurance**, **Regulated Cyber Readiness**, **Intelligent Operations**, **Capability Centres with Embedded IP** |
| Products & Accelerators | "What do you own?" | Eight products and five accelerators (unchanged) |
| Industries | "Do you know my sector?" | Eight industries (unchanged); BFSI explained as "Banking, Insurance & Fintech" |
| Company | "Who are you?" | About Linkfields, Our approach, Insights (blog and news combined), Contact |
| Careers | — | Direct link (unchanged) |
| CTA button | — | "Discuss your challenge" |

The alternative is to drop "Solutions" entirely and run with five menus. Repurposing it is recommended, because it puts the deck's message, *"We lead with outcomes, not offerings"*, directly into the navigation. Each value play first links to its section on `/services`, and later to its own landing page.

The footer should follow the same model. Replace the legacy "Solutions" column with Products & Accelerators. Rename "Quick links" to "Company". Show ISO and membership badges only with entity, scope and validity confirmed.

## 10. What we need from Linkfields

These items cannot be invented. They are what will make the site credible and distinctive.

1. **Two to five case studies** (named or anonymised): challenge, portfolios used, Linkfields IP or accelerator used, outcome, period and publication permission. This is the single biggest lift.
2. **Product screenshots or short demo clips** for each of the eight products, plus deployment and licensing facts (SaaS, licence, on-premises). Clarify **AI Governance**: what is Linkfields-owned and what are third-party tools (Portkey, Opik).
3. **Evidence for credentials:** the source for "#35 FT & Statista Africa Growth Ranking 2026", the current B-BBEE Level 1 certificate, ISO certificate scope and validity, and current NASSCOM, Great Place to Work and YES status.
4. **One confirmed set of company facts:** legal entity name ("(Pvt) Ltd" or "(Pty) Ltd"), the founding year (2008) versus "20+ years of leadership experience", people, client and partner counts, and the office list (Botswana appears on Contact; the UK is in the deck). Also confirm the phone numbers, which conflict.
5. **Partner designations** (SAP, Microsoft, Salesforce, Odoo, Zoho, AWS, Azure), if you want "Partner" rather than "Platforms we deliver".
6. **Leadership team:** names, roles, short biographies and photos for About.
7. **Public LinkedIn URL**, an enquiry response commitment (the "within 24 hours" promise needs confirming), the sales mailbox, and current vacancies or a jobs link.
8. **Legal review** of the Privacy, Cookies and Terms pages. They are currently generic templates.
9. **Insights:** three to five current articles from your specialists (or approval for us to draft them), one per value play.
