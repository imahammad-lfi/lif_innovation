# Accelerator pages review

**Group:** `accelerators` · **Date:** 8 November 2026 · **Verdict:** Rework all five pages; retain all five URLs and the Products & Accelerators taxonomy.

## Summary

The five offers match the corporate deck and their copy is generally restrained, readable and in British English. The fundamental gap is commercial: **“WHAT’S INSIDE” describes capabilities, not the pre-built delivery IP a buyer receives.** None explains what is reused, what must be adapted, what the client must provide, how reuse changes the route to first value, or the applicable engagement model. Consequently, these pages resemble service/product brochures rather than evidence of Linkfields’ reusable IP.

Keep the unified tile styling and sticky section navigation. Differentiate through tangible artefacts, readiness requirements, delivery gates and a relevant example—not five unrelated visual identities. Replace the repeated four-card sibling catalogue with a short, tailored route into portfolios, industries and one or two complementary accelerators. Merge the blue CTA into the local enquiry section. The shared form needs P1 attention: it conflates accelerators with “licence / SaaS” and its client-side validator accepts only South African telephone formats.

**Evidence basis:** The review brief, corporate deck, all five complete page JSON files, rendered HTML via HTTP GET and screenshot inspection. No site content, build, seed, service or form submission was changed. Existing positions below are the original 1-based JSON array indices; “new” rows specify insertion before that existing position. Empty contact/social fields inherit the quoted global wording.

## Group-level findings

### P1 — Make accelerator status and reuse demonstrable

Use the deck’s distinction consistently: **“Software we own. Accelerators we reuse.”** A product may be licensed independently; an accelerator is **“Pre-built delivery IP that shortens time to value.”** Do not infer that accelerators have the same licence/subscription/SaaS route as products. The client must confirm commercial availability, ownership, handover and reuse rights for each offer.

The standard narrative should be: enterprise decision/problem → actual pre-built assets and adaptation boundary → readiness → accepted delivery gates → example/proof → connected services → focused enquiry. Keep all five pages; do not merge Power BI with Predictive Analytics. Explain their complementary model-versus-reporting roles. Do not move Machine learning out of Data & AI or rename the fixed five portfolios.

### P1 — Correct shared conversion obstacles

The rendered form includes **“Products & accelerators (licence / SaaS)”**, making accelerator buying ambiguous. Create a distinct accelerator-delivery route and identify the current accelerator automatically. Source-path tracking already exists; preserve it rather than claiming context is entirely absent.

Read-only inspection of `contact-form-client.tsx` confirms the mandatory phone validator accepts only a local `0` plus nine digits or `+27` plus nine digits. Its error is **“Please enter a valid phone number (e.g. 0xxxxxxxxx or +27xxxxxxxxx)”**. Support international formats with appropriate country handling, or make telephone optional; have the global owner align server validation too. No form was submitted during this review.

Replace **“Let’s connect over a virtual coffee”** with the relevant assessment invitation. Correct **“If required, we sign on a NDA with you, to assure the highest level of privacy.”** to **“Where required, we can agree an NDA before reviewing confidential information.”** An NDA is not a guarantee of the “highest level of privacy”. Confirm **“Our sales team will get in touch within 24 hours”** operationally or remove the fixed promise. Add a concise privacy notice and visible privacy-policy link beside the form, persistent visible labels and a multi-line enquiry field. Do not request confidential client datasets in an initial lead form.

### P1 — Launch metadata and public-link checks

All five URLs return HTTP 200, one relevant H1, unique meaningful titles and relevant meta descriptions. Preserve these strengths. All five reviewed renderings currently emit canonical URLs on the temporary `3001-…manus.computer` forwarding hostname; the JSON canonical fields are empty and `noIndex` is false. **This is an environment/launch finding, not a claim about the existing public website.** Set client-approved public-domain canonicals for launch and prevent indexing of preview hosts.

The inherited LinkedIn destination is **`https://www.linkedin.com/company/40661516/admin/feed/posts/`**. It is an administrative destination, not an appropriate public company link. Have the global owner obtain/confirm the public URL and correct both footer and inherited social defaults; no external navigation/search was performed.

### P2 — Stop repeating the sibling catalogue

Every JSON contains the same five-item `products.accelerator-grid`; **the renderer correctly filters out the current page**, leaving four cards. Do not treat the current accelerator as a visible self-link bug. The remaining problem is four full feature lists on every detail page: they repeat the hub and consume more attention than a one-line relationship warrants. Keep the comprehensive catalogue on `/products-and-accelerators`; detail pages need tailored portfolio/industry links and a maximum of one or two complementary accelerator tiles.

Likewise, retain the client-requested dark eight-industry photo grid in its appropriate hub/company locations, not duplicated wholesale here. The selected contextual industry links should describe potential use cases, not imply unprovided client references.

### P2 — Prove time-to-value without borrowing corporate numbers

The delivery sections are decision-led, a good foundation. Add named outputs, acceptance gates, the reused artefact at each stage and dependencies that determine timing. Do not transplant **“Up to 6×”**, **“Up to 40%”** or the corporate success rate onto individual accelerator pages without suite-specific evidence. A credible first-deliverable definition is better than an invented number of weeks. Replace the shared **“Proven packs and playbooks that de-risk every stage of delivery.”** unless its scope and evidence can be substantiated; “every stage” is unnecessarily absolute.

### P2 — Preserve visual coherence; replace decoration with inspectable IP

The desktop hero → light overview → dark tiles → numbered approach rhythm is clean and the imagery is broadly relevant; the twin equipment image is the strongest contextual match. The common navy tile treatment should remain. The repeated second grid and two blue closing bands, not the shared visual system itself, are the main rhythm problem. Remove/condense those before adding more material. A readable approved model output, twin scenario, workflow, report or release-evidence example would distinguish each offer more effectively than another abstract hero image.

## Missing content the client should supply

1. **Verified reusable-IP inventories:** real model types/assets for prediction and simulation; workflow/orchestration/integration assets; Power BI reporting/integration assets; actual delivery packs and sample evidence. Include version/support status and what remains bespoke. Do not invent template names, connectors or supported platforms.
2. **Commercial and ownership boundaries:** availability by services engagement or other confirmed route, licence dependencies, client access/handover rights, maintenance and support responsibility. Keep software licence/subscription terms separate from accelerator adoption.
3. **Technical readiness and secure-by-design requirements:** data/history/labels, system access, hosting/integration, refresh and latency constraints, permissions, model validation, human approval, audit evidence and acceptance criteria appropriate to the accelerator.
4. **One approved example per accelerator:** redacted actual artefact or clearly labelled demonstration; permission for any client attribution. Supply outcomes with scope, baseline, measurement period and comparator. Do not manufacture clients, numbers, certifications, partner badges or screenshots presented as actual software.
5. **Time-to-value evidence:** first usable deliverable, reused versus new work, delivery assumptions and a documented comparison. Publish a range or improvement metric only when supported by client records.
6. **Relationship confirmation:** how these accelerators connect to portfolios, the Intelligent Operations/Programme Assurance/Regulated Cyber Readiness/Embedded-IP GCC value plays, and relevant proprietary products without implied bundling.
