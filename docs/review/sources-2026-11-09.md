# Verified sources for the Linkfields website review

Verified on 9 November 2026. Each claim is paired with verbatim supporting wording from an opened source; HTML styling and PDF line wrapping are omitted. A null JSON year means the source does not state its publication year. Living-document update years are labelled explicitly and are not presented as original publication dates.

## 1. Independent B2B research and supplier contact

**Claim:** Gartner's survey of 632 B2B buyers found that most prefer to research independently through digital channels, supporting investment in a useful self-service website. [1]

**Exact supporting text:**

> A survey of 632 B2B buyers conducted in August through September 2024 found most buyers prefer to carry out independent research through digital channels.

**Source:** [Gartner Sales Survey Finds 61% of B2B Buyers Prefer a Rep-Free Buying Experience](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)

**Publisher:** Gartner  
**Year:** 2025

**Claim:** Gartner reported in March 2026 that 67% of B2B buyers preferred a rep-free experience, reinforcing the value of clear online information before sales contact. [2]

**Exact supporting text:**

> Buyers want to engage on their own terms as 67% of B2B buyers state that they prefer a rep‑free experience, according to a survey by Gartner, Inc., a business and technology insights company.

**Source:** [Gartner Sales Survey Finds 67% of B2B Buyers Prefer a Rep-Free Experience](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)

**Publisher:** Gartner  
**Year:** 2026

**Citation note:** The widely quoted 17% supplier-meeting-time figure was not recovered from an accessible Gartner primary page. The former journey URLs returned an error or redirected to Gartner’s marketing homepage, so that statistic is deliberately omitted. The two press releases provide independently verified, dated primary evidence instead.

## 6. Visual design, consistency and credibility

**Claim:** Stanford's credibility research supports professional, purpose-appropriate visual design and consistent layout, typography and imagery because people rapidly judge sites by appearance. [10]

**Exact supporting text:**

> We find that people quickly evaluate a site by visual design alone. When designing your site, pay attention to layout, typography, images, consistency issues, and more.

**Source:** [Stanford Guidelines for Web Credibility](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/)

**Publisher:** Stanford Persuasive Technology Lab, Stanford University  
**Year:** 2002

**Claim:** Attractive interfaces can increase perceived usability, although visual polish does not remedy serious usability problems. [11]

**Exact supporting text:**

> The aesthetic-usability effect refers to users’ tendency to perceive attractive products as more usable. People tend to believe that things that look better will work better — even if they aren’t actually more effective or efficient.

**Citation note:** The Stanford guidelines are dated May 2002 and were updated in June 2002; NN/g displays a February 2024 publication date and a September 2026 review date. Visual appeal complements functionality rather than replacing it.

## 9. Speed, Core Web Vitals and business outcomes

**Claim:** Vodafone's controlled landing-page test found that a 31% improvement in Largest Contentful Paint produced 8% more sales and a 15% improvement in lead-to-visit rate, demonstrating a measurable business benefit from faster rendering. [16]

**Exact supporting text:**

> By running an A/B test specifically focused on optimizing Web Vitals, Vodafone found that a 31% improvement in LCP led to 8% more sales, a 15% improvement in their lead to visit rate, and a 11% improvement in their cart to visit rate.

**Source:** [Vodafone: A 31% improvement in LCP increased sales by 8%](https://web.dev/case-studies/vodafone)

**Publisher:** Google web.dev  
**Year:** 2021

**Claim:** The Google-commissioned Deloitte study associated a 0.1-second improvement in mobile site speed with an 8.3% improvement in bounce rate on lead-generation information pages. [17]

**Exact supporting text:**

> Lead generation information pages bounce rate improved by 8.3%

**Source:** [Milliseconds Make Millions](https://www.deloitte.com/ie/en/services/consulting/research/milliseconds-make-millions.html)

**Publisher:** Deloitte Ireland; commissioned by Google  
**Year:** 2020

**Citation note:** Vodafone’s result is from a controlled A/B test, whereas Milliseconds Make Millions examined correlations in natural speed changes across four speed metrics. Neither is a forecast of Linkfields’s conversion uplift. The full Deloitte report’s lead-generation results included improved funnel progression but decreased overall conversion rates, so the 21.6% form-stage improvement is not presented here as an overall conversion gain.

Google treats redirects and canonical declarations as strong signals, not a promise of unchanged rankings.

## 12. POPIA and European consent

**Claim:** Michalsons advises that using cookies for direct marketing to a person who is not already a customer requires that person's consent under POPIA. [22]

**Exact supporting text:**

> If a responsible party uses cookies for the purpose of direct marketing and the data subject is not a customer of the responsible party, the responsible party must get the data subject’s consent.

**Source:** [Cookie law in South Africa | Guidance and actions](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/cookie-law-south-africa/15264)

**Publisher:** Michalsons  
**Year:** 2019

**Claim:** Under POPIA, electronic direct marketing requires consent unless the recipient qualifies for the conditional existing-customer exception, so enquiry submission should not automatically enrol prospects in marketing. [23]

**Exact supporting text:**

> The processing of personal information of a data subject for the purpose of direct marketing by means of any form of electronic communication, including automatic calling machines, facsimile machines, SMSs or e-mail is prohibited unless the data subject —
> (a) has given his, her or its consent to the processing; or
> (b) is, subject to subsection (3), a customer of the responsible party

**Source:** [Guidance Note on Direct Marketing in terms of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf)

**Publisher:** Information Regulator South Africa  
**Year:** 2024

**Claim:** POPIA direct-marketing communications must identify the sender and provide contact details through which recipients can ask for marketing to stop. [23]

**Exact supporting text:**

> Any communication for the purpose of direct marketing must contain—
> (a) details of the identity of the sender or the person on whose behalf the communication has been sent; and
> (b) an address or other contact details to which the recipient may send a request that such communications cease

**Source:** [Guidance Note on Direct Marketing in terms of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/GUIDANCE-NOTE-ON-DIRECT-MARKETING-IN-TERMS-OF-THE-PROTECTION-OF-PERSONAL-INFORMATION-ACT-4-OF-2013-POPIA.pdf)

**Publisher:** Information Regulator South Africa  
**Year:** 2024

**Claim:** Where GDPR consent is relied upon, pre-ticked boxes, silence and merely continuing to use a service do not constitute valid active consent. [24]

**Exact supporting text:**

> The use of pre-ticked opt-in boxes is invalid under the GDPR. Silence or inactivity on the part of the data subject, as well as merely proceeding with a service cannot be regarded as an active indication of choice.

**Source:** [Guidelines 05/2020 on consent under Regulation 2016/679](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf)

**Publisher:** European Data Protection Board (EDPB)  
**Year:** 2020

**Citation note:** The Information Regulator dates the direct-marketing guidance to 3 December 2024, despite the older upload directory in its URL. Michalsons’s cookie article was first published on 30 August 2019 and updated on 29 November 2022; its statements are law-firm interpretation, not a separate cookie statute. POPIA does not explicitly mention cookies and consent is not the only lawful basis for all processing. The existing-customer marketing exception has conditions, including similar products/services and an opportunity to object at collection and in every communication. European cookie storage/access rules arise from ePrivacy law, while GDPR governs the validity of consent and relevant personal-data processing; the regimes should not be conflated.

## 13. security.txt

**Claim:** RFC 9116 defines security.txt to make vulnerability reporting easier by publishing an organisation's disclosure practices in a machine-readable format. [25]

**Exact supporting text:**

> This document defines a machine-parsable format ("security.txt") to help organizations describe their vulnerability disclosure practices to make it easier for researchers to report vulnerabilities.

**Source:** [RFC 9116: A File Format to Aid in Security Vulnerability Disclosure](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html)

**Publisher:** Internet Engineering Task Force / RFC Editor  
**Year:** 2022

**Claim:** A security.txt file must include a Contact field specifying how researchers should report vulnerabilities. [25]

**Exact supporting text:**

> The "Contact" field indicates a method that researchers should use for reporting security vulnerabilities such as an email address, a phone number, and/or a web page with contact information. This field MUST always be present in a "security.txt" file.

**Source:** [RFC 9116: A File Format to Aid in Security Vulnerability Disclosure](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html)

**Publisher:** Internet Engineering Task Force / RFC Editor  
**Year:** 2022

**Claim:** RFC 9116 requires web-based services to publish security.txt under /.well-known/ so that vulnerability researchers can find it consistently. [25]

**Exact supporting text:**

> For web-based services, organizations MUST place the "security.txt" file under the "/.well-known/" path

**Source:** [RFC 9116: A File Format to Aid in Security Vulnerability Disclosure](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html)

**Publisher:** Internet Engineering Task Force / RFC Editor  
**Year:** 2022

**Citation note:** RFC 9116 was published in April 2022 and is Informational, not an Internet Standards Track specification. It also requires an Expires field, HTTPS access and UTF-8 text/plain content. Publishing security.txt is a disclosure/contact measure, not proof that the site is secure.
